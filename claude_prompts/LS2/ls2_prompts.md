# LS2

## Goal

Bring the LS2 inversion algorithm (Loisel et al. 2018; origin Loisel & Stramski
2000) into IOPtics as a benchmarked algorithm alongside BING, and then
**re-derive its empirical coefficients from our own radiative transfer** rather
than inheriting the published ones.

Why it matters for the thesis.  The ratified thesis statement
(`claudes-phd-thesis/claude_prompts/qual_exam_prompts.md`, A1) is a separation
claim: forward-model (physics) error is identifiable and removable, and the
residual is *parameterization* error concentrated in the decomposition of
absorption into phytoplankton and CDOM/detrital components.  LS2 is the sharpest
available test of the second half, because **LS2 returns total `a`, `anw`, `bb`
and `bbp` and never decomposes absorption at all.**  It buys that freedom with an
extra observable (Kd) and two empirical side-chains.  The prediction is therefore
falsifiable in both directions:

- LS2 should be at least as good as BING on **total** absorption — RT-A already
  shows a(440) is ~5% under all five RT physics, i.e. the totals are the easy
  part.  If LS2's totals are *worse*, the "totals are insensitive" half of the
  claim is wrong.
- LS2 is structurally incapable of producing `a_ph`/`a_dg`.  An algorithm that
  declines to parameterize the decomposition gets the totals and forfeits the
  decomposition: that demonstrates the separation by construction.

The caveat that must not get lost: LS2 is **not** parameterization-free.  It
imports two parameterizations through the side door — Kd from a neural network
and `b_p` from a Chl-a band-ratio algorithm — and both are fitted to data.  Part
of this work is measuring what those cost.

### The two papers

- `claudes-phd-thesis/context/papers/loisel_stramski2000.pdf` — Loisel &
  Stramski 2000, Appl. Opt. 39(18), 3001–3011.  The origin ("LS1").  Inputs are
  irradiance reflectance R(0⁻) and ⟨Kd⟩₁; a from Eq. 11 with h̄(μw) (Eq. 10),
  b from Eq. 13/14, bb from Eq. 17 with α (Eq. 18) and δ (Eq. 19), then a
  fixed-point Raman correction κ (Eqs. 20–22), iterated "until a change in the
  estimated ratio bb/a becomes arbitrarily small — typically less than 0.1%
  after two or three iterations".  The LUTs rest on 528 simulations solved by
  **either invariant embedding (Hydrolight 3.0) or Monte Carlo** — not Monte
  Carlo alone.  The κ table is described but not tabulated, and in LS1 it is
  indexed by **bb/a *and* solar zenith angle**.
- `claudes-phd-thesis/context/papers/Loisel_etal_JGR2018.pdf` — Loisel et al.
  2018, JGR-Oceans 123, 2141–2171.  **This is the version that is implemented.**
  Five changes from 2000: Rrs replaces R(0⁻); ⟨Kd⟩₁ comes from a neural network;
  η comes from a Chl→b_p route instead of from the retrieved b (because LS1's η
  inherited b's strong phase-function dependence); the simulations are
  restricted to realistic (b/a, η) pairs and extended into turbid water; and the
  a-relation now accounts for h's η-dependence, which LS1 neglected.  The closed
  forms h̄/α/δ are replaced by two cubic polynomials in Rrs — Eq. 9 for a, Eq. 8
  for bb — whose coefficients are **all** indexed by (η, μw), not merely the
  leading one.  Fig. 1–2 show the LS1 power law failing above Rrs ≈ 0.01 sr⁻¹
  where the polynomial tracks the RT points.

The 2018 algorithm, step by step (Table 1, §3.2):

1. Inputs Rrs(λ), θs; μw = cos[asin(sin θs / 1.34)].
2. ⟨Kd(λ)⟩₁ from the neural network (or in situ, if available).  ⟨Kd⟩₁ is
   defined (LS1, Table 1) as 1/z₁, where z₁ is the depth at which Ed falls **to**
   1/e ≈ 36.8% of its subsurface value.  The 2018 paper misstates this as
   "reduced *by* 37%"; follow LS1.
3. Chl from OC4v4 → b_p(λ) "using calculations based on Loisel & Morel 1998",
   with the Morel & Maritorena 2001 spectral slope; b = b_p + b_w.  **The 2018
   paper prints neither the relation nor its coefficients** — only those two
   citations — so the formula must be sourced from LM98/MM01 themselves.
4. η = b_w / b.
5. a = ⟨Kd⟩₁ / [a₁(η,μw) + a₂(η,μw) Rrs + a₃(η,μw) Rrs² + a₄(η,μw) Rrs³]  (Eq. 9).
6. a_nw = a − a_w.
7. bb = ⟨Kd⟩₁ [b_b1(η,μw) Rrs + b_b2(η,μw) Rrs² + b_b3(η,μw) Rrs³]  (Eq. 8).
8. bb_p = bb − bb_w, bb_w = b_w/2.
9. Raman: Rrs ← κ(λ, bb/a)·Rrs, re-enter at step 1 and repeat "until a
   convergence for successive estimates of IOPs is achieved".  2018 states no
   numerical criterion; LS1's is the only one published.

A useful limiting check on any re-derived a-LUT: as Rrs → 0, a/⟨Kd⟩₁ → μw, i.e.
a₁(η,μw) → 1/μw.

Derivation basis (§3.1): Hydrolight 5.0, homogeneous infinitely deep ocean,
5 m s⁻¹ wind, clear sky, θs = 0,10,…,70°.  Set S1 (no Raman; builds the a and bb
LUTs) fixes a = 1 m⁻¹, sweeps b/a over **36** values in 0.05–30 and η over 21
values in 0–0.20, with a **single** particle phase function — the Mobley 1994
average of three Petzold VSFs.  Set S2 (the κ correction) uses a Case-1
bio-optical library, Chl ∈ **23** values 0.01–5.0 mg m⁻³, 350–700 nm, with and
without Raman.  Neither set includes chlorophyll or CDOM fluorescence.  The
accepted (b/a, η) pair list is **not published** — only the criterion that
combinations such as high b/a with high η are unrealistic in Case-1 water.  The
a/bb coefficient LUTs and the κ LUT live in Supporting Information S1, which is
not in the PDF we hold.

Stated accuracy (§4): on IOCCG synthetic data with **true** Kd, a has MAPD 7.1%
and bb 8.3% (bb_p 9.8%); with the **NN** Kd both degrade to ≈15% MAPD.  The a_nw
MAPD spectrum must be quoted from **one** row of Table 2 at a time — true Kd:
8.59 / 8.65 / 9.66 / 13.07 / 25.18 / 47.40 % at 410/440/490/510/555/670 nm;
NN Kd: 16.73 / 15.01 / 23.36 / 24.67 / 55.55 / 426.80 %.  The 670 nm blow-up is
structural: a_w is ~99% of a(670) and ~95% of a(550) below Chl 0.1.
In situ: coastal a MAPD 22–31%, bb_p 27–44%; open-ocean/polar a MAPD 7–14%,
bb_p MAPD 28–53%; NOMAD matchups a_nw(443) MAPD 19%, bb_p(443) MAPD 22%.  Those
statistics **exclude negative retrievals**, which the paper counts but does not
handle.  θs 0–70°, clear sky; 400–700 nm is the κ range, while the Kd network is
developed for 412–670 nm.

Two caveats the paper flags that bear directly on Q6: a and bb are only weakly
phase-function sensitive (b is strongly so, which is why LS1's η route was
abandoned), and bb_p is systematically overestimated when bb_p ≲ bb_w
(≲ 0.002 m⁻¹) — a 10% high ⟨Kd⟩₁ there costs ~30% at 443 nm.

### What already exists: `ocpy/ls2/`

A complete, working, tested port — **and it is JXP's, from 2023, not a thesis
contribution.**  577 lines over `ls2_main.py` (307), `kd_nn.py` (242) and
`io.py` (28), shipping `ocpy/data/LS2/LS2_LUT.npz` plus the Kd network weights
(`weights_1.csv`, `weights_2.csv`, `train_switch.csv`).  Lineage in the header:
C by Dessailly 2018 → MATLAB by Jorge 2020 → Kehrli 2022 → Kehrli, Reynolds &
Stramski 2022 → "Converted by Python by JXP and Claude+, 2023-06-22", with a
2023-10-10 correction to the clear-water Kd weights.  Both tests
(`ocpy/tests/test_ls2.py`, `test_ls2_kd.py`) pass in `ocean14` — but see the
coverage caveat below.

`LS2_LUT.npz` holds **five** arrays *(regenerated)*: `a` (21, 8, 4),
`bb` (21, 8, 3), `eta` (21, **1**), `muw` (8, **1**) and `kappa` (101, 7); the
code flattens the two grid vectors before use.  The η grid is non-uniform
(steps of 0.005, 0.01 and 0.05; 0.010 itself is absent) and `muw` reproduces
cos[asin(sin θs/1.34)] for θs = 0…70° in 10° steps to 5×10⁻⁷.  Interpolation is
bilinear in (η, μw) — but of the *derived* a and bb at the four corner nodes,
not of the coefficients, via `RegularGridInterpolator` on the bracketing 2×2
cell.

**The port departs from the paper in five places, and has one outright defect.**
These are the things a benchmark must not silently inherit:

1. **`b_p` is an input, not computed.**  `LS2_main(sza, lambda_, Rrs, Kd, aw,
   bw, bp, LS2_LUT, Flag_Raman)` takes `bp` from the caller; the comment at
   `ls2_main.py:64` says so and notes that in Loisel et al. 2018 `b_p` is
   estimated from Chl via OC4v4.  So the operational step-3 chain is absent.
   `ocpy.chl.band_ratios.oc4` exists (`band_ratios.py:30`, OC4v4 coefficients,
   nearest-band lookup of 443/490/510/555); a Chl→b_p relation exists **nowhere**
   in ocpy — the only scattering code there is pure-seawater (Zhang-Hu-He 2009)
   and per-particle cross-sections.
2. **The Kd network is a MODIS-band variant**, not the architecture described in
   the paper.  `Kd_NN_MODIS` takes five Rrs values plus sza and λ, switches on
   `Rrs[1]/Rrs[3] ≥ 0.85`, and has hidden layers of 8/6 (clear, which drops the
   red band → 6 inputs) and 9/6 (turbid, 7 inputs) — layer sizes confirmed from
   the weight files.  The paper describes a switch on Rrs(490)/Rrs(555) with
   2×5 and 2×4 neurons over bands 443–670 nm.  Output λ is free (λ is a
   normalised network input, with no range check); input bands are fixed — and
   **positional**: the code never inspects wavelengths, so "443, 488, 531, 547,
   667 nm" and the "40,000 training samples" are docstring assertions only
   (`kd_nn.py:67-68, 81-82`), unsupported by anything shipped.
3. **The Raman correction runs once**, not to convergence (paper step 9).
4. **…and that single pass is wrong for `a`.**  After `Rrs ← κ·Rrs`, only the
   `a00` corner is recomputed; where `a01`, `a10`, `a11` should follow,
   `ls2_main.py:155` carries the literal placeholder
   `#... (similar calculations for a01, a10, a11)`, so the bilinear
   interpolation mixes one corrected corner with three stale ones.  `bb` does
   recompute all four.  Against the authors' own reference vector over the 46
   cells where κ is finite *(regenerated)*: `a` is off by up to **1.03%** (and
   `a_nw`, as a small difference, by up to ~45%), while recomputing all four
   corners reproduces the reference to 4×10⁻¹⁵.  This is a bug to fix, not a
   departure to preserve.
5. **The shipped κ table is a cubic in bb/a over 302–702 nm at 4 nm**
   *(regenerated)*, shape (101, 7): λ, c₃, c₂, c₁, c₀ and the valid bb/a range
   (0.0007–0.4483), with c₃..c₁ = 0 and c₀ = 1 below 326 nm.  The paper gives
   400–700 nm, no functional form, and — unlike LS1 — **no θs index**.  Outside
   the tabulated bb/a range the code warns and sets κ = NaN; outside the
   tabulated λ range `np.interp` silently **clamps**, despite a docstring
   promising extrapolation.

Integration hazards beyond the five departures:

- **The `ocpy` tests do not really pin the port.**  `test_ls2.py` asserts only
  `bb` at 412 nm (10 numbers, rtol 1e-3) — precisely the output the defect above
  leaves untouched — and `test_ls2_kd.py` never opens the reference CSV, only two
  hard-coded scalars.  The reference vector itself is richer: 60 rows (6
  wavelengths × 10 samples) × 13 columns, with all five outputs.
- **`LS2_main` is strictly scalar**: one sample × one wavelength per call (array
  input raises), and it returns a plain tuple `(a, anw, bb, bbp, kappa)`.
- **Off-grid inputs return `None`**, not NaN, so tuple-unpacking callers crash;
  and a value landing exactly on the last grid node (η = 0.2, or θs = 70.0°)
  raises `UnboundLocalError`.  The usable envelope is 0 ≤ θs < 70° and
  0 ≤ b_w/(b_p+b_w) < 0.2, i.e. **b_p > 4·b_w** — which clear blue water fails.
- Negative a/a_nw/bb/bb_p are silently replaced by NaN (the authors' reference
  keeps them negative); `Flag_Raman` is tested for truthiness, not equality to 1;
  a NaN κ is returned as NaN rather than 1.
- `from IPython import embed` at module import in both modules, and `pkg_resources`
  (deprecated) for the data paths.  `Kd_NN_MODIS` re-reads all three CSVs on
  every call and returns a `(1,1)` array.
- The ocpy Sphinx pages (`docs/api/ls2.rst`, quickstart, user guide) document an
  array/dict API that does not exist, and state the LUT covers θs 0/30/60.
  Distrust them; read the code.

### Can it run on our corpora?

Yes on L23, with truth.  LS2 needs Kd averaged over the first attenuation depth,
which L23's main file does not carry — but `Hydrolight{X}{Y}_profile.nc` has
`KEd_z(z, IOP_Scenario, Lambda)` and `z1_lambda(Lambda, IOP_Scenario)` (note the
transposed order), so ⟨Kd⟩₁ is a depth-average away.  Recomputed for X=4, Y=00
over the whole corpus *(regenerated)*: 266,067 of 268,920 (scenario, wavelength)
pairs finite (98.94%), 3,082 of 3,320 with a complete spectrum, median ⟨Kd⟩₁ of
0.035, 0.032, 0.070 and 0.459 m⁻¹ at 440, 490, 555 and 670 nm — the 670 value
tracking a_w, as it should.  The 2,853 missing pairs are the `-999` fills of
`z1_lambda`, all between 355 and 490 nm, where z₁ is deeper than the 50 m grid.

Two cautions on that average:

- **These numbers are θs = 0 only.**  The solar zenith angle is encoded solely in
  the filename digits `YY`; there is no sza variable and the global attributes
  are empty.  θs ∈ {0, 30, 60} means loading `Hydrolight{X}00/30/60`, and
  IOPtics already recovers θs from the load option (`run.resolve_theta_s`).
- **`z1_lambda` is internally inconsistent with `Ed_z`, so `1/z1` is not a valid
  shortcut for ⟨Kd⟩₁** *(regenerated)*.  Against the e-folding depth implied by
  the stored `Ed_z`, `z1_lambda` is 1.77× too deep at 700 nm (ratio 0.565), with
  similar excursions at 595–615, 645–655 and 690–740 nm; 37,039 pairs differ by
  more than 10%, and the median ⟨Kd⟩₁/(1/z₁) reaches 1.76 at the p99.  The same
  pattern is in X=1.  The trapezoidal average over `[0, z1]` survives this
  (L23's ocean is homogeneous, so the average changes by ≲1.6% if z₁ is replaced
  by the Ed-derived depth), but any future use of `z1_lambda` itself needs the
  caveat.

L23 also supplies scattering truth for `b_p`: the right variable is **`bnw`**
(= `bph + bd` to 9e-5; `bph` alone is not b_p).  All nine X/Y combinations
exist locally as main+profile pairs, so X=1, 2 and 4 are all available —
**but `ioptics/datasets.py:158-160` raises `ValueError` on X=2** ("not used by
IOPtics; use X=1 or X=4"), so the κ re-derivation of Q6 must either go through
`ocpy.hydrolight.loisel23.load_ds` directly or lift that restriction.

PANGAEA carries **measured** Kd, but less usefully than the original Context
implied.  `ocpy/insitu/pangaea.py:41` maps `KD` → `kd` and ocpy's default
`iop_kinds` includes it — but IOPtics' `_PANGAEA_TRUTH_KINDS`
(`datasets.py:200-204`) carries only `aph`, `acdom`, `bbp`, so Kd never reaches
`RawObs.truth` today.  The Kd itself is **multispectral only**: 2,454 of 7,424
rows have any Kd over 25 discrete wavelengths, 1,998 have ≥5 bands, and **none**
pairs with a hyperspectral Rrs.  The metadata asserts nothing about a
depth-averaging convention, so it is not claimed to be ⟨Kd⟩₁.  GLORIA has no Kd
at all.  For PACE, the artifact the IOPtics adapter expects
(`$OS_COLOR/IOPtics/pace_pab_100/pace_pab_100.parquet`) is **not on this
machine**, no local PACE product carries Kd, and the one L3m composite that does
exist has no per-pixel sza — which the Kd network requires.

### Where LS2 does not fit IOPtics

`ioptics/algorithms/spec.py:158-224`'s `AlgorithmSpec` is built entirely around
parameterized least squares: `anw_model`, `bbnw_model`, `apriors`, `bpriors`,
`othera_priors`, `fit_method`, `mcmc`, `maxfev`, `beta` — plus `rt`, `set_Sdg`,
`sSdg` and `fits_turbid`.  Its own docstring says it: "An IOPtics 'algorithm' is
a configuration, not code."  LS2 has none of that.  It is closed-form, solves
every wavelength independently, reproduces the input Rrs exactly by construction
(so there is **no misfit, no χ²_ν, no ΔBIC and no posterior coverage**), and it
takes an observable BING never sees.  This is the first non-BING-shaped
algorithm the framework has been asked to hold.

What the code actually constrains, for the `DirectSpec` of Q1:

- `registry.register(spec, *, overwrite=False)` is **type-agnostic** — a
  `DirectSpec` can go through the same call, so a `register_direct()` is a
  stylistic choice, not a technical requirement.  The one-`register()` rule
  lives in the prompt docs, not in code, and the RT variants already bent it.
- The real branch point is `run.run_algorithm` (`run.py:648-664`), which
  dispatches on `fit_method` and raises on anything unknown; `run_sweep:1083`
  hard-codes `fit_method='chisq'` for the batch pass.  `config.ALLOWED_FIT_METHODS`
  (`config.py:55`) gates the config, and `provenance.algorithm_block`
  (`provenance.py:85-141`) reads BING-only fields unguarded and will raise on a
  `DirectSpec`.  `evaluate._assemble` is bound to BING model objects and is not
  reusable; LS2 needs its own result builder.  `io.py` and `records.py` need no
  change if the result reuses `RetrievalResult`.
- **`evaluate._fit_status` gates `'ok'` on a finite χ²_ν ≤ 5**, so a NaN χ²_ν
  scores as `poor_fit`.  LS2's builder must set `status` itself (per Q5's rule).
- Several metrics do not go NaN for LS2 — they go to a **misleading zero**:
  `frac_qc_fail` is `mean(chi2_nu > 5)` (NaN > 5 is False → 0.0), and
  `frac_good`/`frac_overfit`/`frac_underfit` all read 0.0 via an `'unknown'`
  quality label.  `rel_misfit` would be exactly **0**, not NaN, if LS2 emits an
  `Rrs_model` component.  ΔBIC rows drop out silently (n=0 → `continue`), with
  no explicit N/A.  Coverage does go cleanly NaN if the bounds are NaN.
- Metric names, for the record: it is **`coverage68`**, not `cov68`; the
  metrics-stage columns are `chi2_nu_median`, `rel_misfit_median` and
  `rel_misfit_median_all` (`chi2_nu` and `rel_misfit` are the per-fit columns in
  `results_scalar`).
- **`a_nw` is not a component the framework knows.**  `evaluate._SPECTRAL`,
  `metrics.ACCURACY_COMPONENTS`, `_COMPONENT_REFSET` and `io._UNITS` all carry
  the same five: `a`, `bb`, `a_ph`, `a_dg`, `bb_p` (+`Rrs_model`).  A head-to-head
  on `a`, `a_nw`, `bb`, `bb_p` therefore needs `a_nw` added to all four **and**
  BING taught to emit `a_dg + a_ph` as `a_nw`.
- **θs is already solved**: `run.resolve_theta_s` (`run.py:331-383`) serves every
  dataset (L23 from `meta['Y']`, the rest from `robust.solar.solar_zenith`), and
  it is spec-independent — LS2 can call it directly.  **Kd is not**: there is no
  slot for a second observable anywhere in `RawObs` (`datasets.py:32-73`) or
  `PreparedRecord` (`records.py:141-154`), so one must be added (≈3 edits) rather
  than smuggled through `meta` or `truth`.
- Report panels are data-driven (`standard._plan_panels` from
  `figures.scored_refs`), so LS2 will not break them — it will just be **silently
  absent** from `a_ph`/`a_dg` rows.  The explicit "not applicable" rows Q5 asks
  for go in `metrics._ref_accuracy_rows` / `_scalar_var_rows` as n=0 rows with a
  new caveat value.
- Test tiers (`ioptics/tests/conftest.py`) are `@needs_data`, `@needs_l23`,
  `@needs_pangaea`, `@needs_gloria`, `@needs_pace_pab` and `@needs_inelastic`.
  Note that **`@needs_pace` guards ocpy's PACE *noise table***, not the dataset —
  the dataset guard is `@needs_pace_pab`.  Nothing yet probes
  `Hydrolight{X}{Y}_profile.nc`, which is where ⟨Kd⟩₁ lives, so an LS2 tier needs
  a new guard.

**Q1. How far may the one-`register()` rule bend?**  The convention says adding
an algorithm is one `register(...)` call with no core changes.  LS2 cannot be
that: it is closed-form, needs Kd as a second observable, and produces no
misfit, so `fit_method`, `mcmc`, `apriors`/`bpriors` and every χ²-derived metric
are meaningless for it.  Three ways out, in increasing order of honesty and
cost: (a) a shim that wraps LS2 in a fake one-parameter "fit" so the existing
pipeline runs unchanged — cheap, and it will produce nonsense in the χ² columns;
(b) a second spec type (`DirectSpec`) and a `register_direct()` entry point,
with the run stage branching once on which kind it has and the metrics stage
emitting NaN for the fit-quality family — a real but contained core change;
(c) a separate runner and report for non-fitting algorithms, which duplicates
the metrics code.  *Recommended:* (b).  It is the change that makes IOPtics a
benchmarking framework rather than a BING harness, and the thesis wants exactly
that comparison.

>A. Go with (b)

**Q2. `b_p` is the hidden parameterization — which rung of the ladder do we
run?**  `ocpy`'s LS2 takes `b_p` as an argument; operational LS2 derives it from
Chl via OC4v4.  On L23 we can hand it truth.  That gives three rungs:
(i) **true Kd + true b_p** — what LS2 can do at its best, and an upper bound
nobody could reach from orbit; (ii) **true Kd + OC4v4 b_p** — isolates the cost
of the Chl side-chain; (iii) **NN Kd + OC4v4 b_p** — satellite mode, the only
rung that is a fair comparison with BING, which sees Rrs alone.  *Recommended:*
all three, on L23, reported as a ladder.  The steps between the rungs are the
result — they say how much of LS2's advertised accuracy is really an accuracy
of its inputs.  This needs a Chl→b_p function (Loisel & Morel 1998 with the
Morel & Maritorena 2001 slope) added to `ocpy`, which does not have one.  Do you
want it in `ocpy` or local to IOPtics?

>A. Sure, let's do all 3.  I have generated an `ls2` branch on `ocpy`.  We will use that

**Q4. Which L23 inelastic variant, and do we run more than one?**  RT-A used

**Q5. What does "ok" mean for an algorithm with no misfit?**  The metrics stage

# LS2

## Goal

Bring the LS2 inversion algorithm (Loisel et al. 2018; origin Loisel & Stramski
2000) into IOPtics as a benchmarked algorithm alongside BING, and then
**re-derive its empirical coefficients from our own radiative transfer** rather
than inheriting the published ones.

Why it matters for the thesis.  The ratified thesis statement
(`claudes-phd-thesis/claude_prompts/qual_exam_prompts.md`, A1) is a separation
claim: forward-model (physics) error is identifiable and removable, and the
residual is *parameterization* error concentrated in the decomposition of
absorption into phytoplankton and CDOM/detrital components.  LS2 is the sharpest
available test of the second half, because **LS2 returns total `a`, `anw`, `bb`
and `bbp` and never decomposes absorption at all.**  It buys that freedom with an
extra observable (Kd) and two empirical side-chains.  The prediction is therefore
falsifiable in both directions:

- LS2 should be at least as good as BING on **total** absorption — RT-A already
  shows a(440) is ~5% under all five RT physics, i.e. the totals are the easy
  part.  If LS2's totals are *worse*, the "totals are insensitive" half of the
  claim is wrong.
- LS2 is structurally incapable of producing `a_ph`/`a_dg`.  An algorithm that
  declines to parameterize the decomposition gets the totals and forfeits the
  decomposition: that demonstrates the separation by construction.

The caveat that must not get lost: LS2 is **not** parameterization-free.  It
imports two parameterizations through the side door — Kd from a neural network
and `b_p` from a Chl-a band-ratio algorithm — and both are fitted to data.  Part
of this work is measuring what those cost.

### The two papers

- `claudes-phd-thesis/context/papers/loisel_stramski2000.pdf` — Loisel &
  Stramski 2000, Appl. Opt. 39(18), 3001–3011.  The origin ("LS1").  Inputs are
  irradiance reflectance R(0⁻) and ⟨Kd⟩₁; a from Eq. 11 with h̄(μw) (Eq. 10),
  b from Eq. 13/14, bb from Eq. 17 with α (Eq. 18) and δ (Eq. 19), then a
  fixed-point Raman correction κ (Eq. 21).  LUTs from 528 Morel & Loisel 1998
  Monte Carlo runs; the κ table is described but not tabulated.
- `claudes-phd-thesis/context/papers/Loisel_etal_JGR2018.pdf` — Loisel et al.
  2018, JGR-Oceans 123, 2141–2171.  **This is the version that is implemented.**
  Four changes from 2000: Rrs replaces R(0⁻); ⟨Kd⟩₁ comes from a neural network;
  η comes from a Chl→b_p route instead of from the retrieved b (because LS1's η
  was strongly phase-function dependent); and the simulations are restricted to
  realistic (b/a, η) pairs and extended into turbid water.  The closed forms
  h̄/α/δ are replaced by two cubic polynomials in Rrs with (η, μw)-indexed
  coefficients — Eq. 9 for a, Eq. 8 for bb.  Fig. 1–2 show the LS1 power law
  failing above Rrs ≈ 0.01 sr⁻¹ where the polynomial tracks the RT points.

The 2018 algorithm, step by step (Table 1, §3.2):

1. Inputs Rrs(λ), θs; μw = cos[asin(sin θs / 1.34)].
2. ⟨Kd(λ)⟩₁ from the neural network (or in situ, if available).
3. Chl from OC4v4 → b_p(λ) via Loisel & Morel 1998 with the Morel & Maritorena
   2001 spectral slope; b = b_p + b_w.
4. η = b_w / b.
5. a = ⟨Kd⟩₁ / [a₁(η,μw) + a₂ Rrs + a₃ Rrs² + a₄ Rrs³]  (Eq. 9).
6. a_nw = a − a_w.
7. bb = ⟨Kd⟩₁ [b_b1(η,μw) Rrs + b_b2 Rrs² + b_b3 Rrs³]  (Eq. 8).
8. bb_p = bb − bb_w, bb_w = b_w/2.
9. Raman: Rrs ← κ(λ, bb/a)·Rrs and iterate to convergence.

Derivation basis (§3.1): Hydrolight 5.0, homogeneous infinitely deep ocean,
5 m s⁻¹ wind, clear sky, θs = 0,10,…,70°.  Set S1 (no Raman; builds the a and bb
LUTs) fixes a = 1 m⁻¹, sweeps b/a over 37 values in 0.05–30 and η over 21 values
in 0–0.20, with a **single** particle phase function — the Mobley 1994 average of
three Petzold VSFs.  Set S2 (the κ correction) uses a Case-1 bio-optical library,
Chl ∈ 18 values 0.01–5.0 mg m⁻³, 350–700 nm, with and without Raman.  Neither set
includes chlorophyll or CDOM fluorescence.

Stated accuracy (§4): on IOCCG synthetic data with **true** Kd, a has MAPD 7.1%
and bb 8.3%; with the **NN** Kd both degrade to ≈15% MAPD.  a_nw MAPD runs 8.6%
at 410 nm → 55.5% at 555 → 427% at 670 (clear water, where a_w is 95–99% of a).
In situ: coastal a MAPD 22–31%, bb_p 27–44%; open-ocean/polar a MAPD 7–14%,
bb_p MAPD 28–53%; NOMAD matchups a_nw(443) MAPD 19%, bb_p(443) MAPD 22%.
Domain 400–700 nm, θs 0–70°, clear sky.

### What already exists: `ocpy/ls2/`

A complete, working, tested port — **and it is JXP's, from 2023, not a thesis
contribution.**  577 lines over `ls2_main.py`, `kd_nn.py` and `io.py`, shipping
`ocpy/data/LS2/LS2_LUT.npz` and the Kd network weights.  Lineage in the header:
C by Dessailly 2018 → MATLAB by Jorge 2020 → Kehrli, Reynolds & Stramski 2022 →
"Converted by Python by JXP and Claude+, 2023-06-22".  Both tests
(`ocpy/tests/test_ls2.py`, `test_ls2_kd.py`) pass in `ocean14` against the
authors' own reference vector `ocpy/tests/files/LS2_test_run.csv`.

LUT shapes verified against the paper's grids: `a` (21, 8, 4), `bb` (21, 8, 3),
`eta` (21,), `muw` (8,) — 21 η nodes, 8 solar-zenith nodes (θs = 0…70° in 10°
steps), bilinear interpolation between them.

**Four places the port departs from the paper.**  These are the things a
benchmark must not silently inherit:

1. **`b_p` is an input, not computed.**  `LS2_main(sza, lambda_, Rrs, Kd, aw,
   bw, bp, LS2_LUT, Flag_Raman)` takes `bp` from the caller; the code comment at
   `ls2_main.py:64` says so and notes that in Loisel et al. 2018 `b_p` is
   estimated from Chl via OC4v4.  So the operational step-3 chain is absent.
   `ocpy.chl.band_ratios.oc4` exists; **the Chl→b_p relation does not exist
   anywhere in ocpy** and would have to be added.
2. **The Kd network is a MODIS-band variant**, not the architecture described in
   the paper.  `Kd_NN_MODIS` takes Rrs at 443, 488, 531, 547 and 667 nm plus
   sza, switches on Rrs(488)/Rrs(547) ≥ 0.85, and has hidden layers of 8/6 and
   9/6 trained on 40,000 samples.  The paper describes a switch on
   Rrs(490)/Rrs(555) with 2×5 and 2×4 neurons and bands 443–670.  Output λ is
   free; input bands are not.
3. **The Raman correction runs once**, not to convergence (paper step 9).
4. **The shipped κ table is a cubic in bb/a over 302–702 nm at 4 nm**
   (shape (101, 7): λ, c₃, c₂, c₁, c₀, and the valid bb/a range), where the
   paper states 400–700 nm and does not give the functional form.  The code
   warns and sets κ = NaN outside the tabulated bb/a range.

### Can it run on our corpora?

Yes on L23, with truth.  LS2 needs Kd averaged over the first attenuation depth,
which L23's main file does not carry — but `Hydrolight{X}00_profile.nc` has
`KEd_z(z, IOP_Scenario, Lambda)` and the per-wavelength `z1_lambda`, so ⟨Kd⟩₁ is
a depth-average away.  Computed for X=4 over the whole corpus
(`claudes-phd-thesis/claudes_phd_thesis/scripts/ls2_feasibility.py`, report
`reports/ls2_feasibility.md`): 266,067 of 268,920 (scenario, wavelength) pairs
finite (98.9%), 3,082 of 3,320 with a complete spectrum, median ⟨Kd⟩₁ of 0.035,
0.032, 0.070 and 0.459 m⁻¹ at 440, 490, 555 and 670 nm — the 670 value tracking
a_w, as it should.  L23 also supplies `bnw`/`bph` as truth for `b_p`, and θs ∈
{0, 30, 60}.

PANGAEA carries **measured** Kd (`ocpy/insitu/pangaea.py` maps `KD` → `kd`, and
`iop_kinds` already includes it), so a real-data arm is possible.  GLORIA has no
Kd loaded and would need the network or an empirical Kd.

### Where LS2 does not fit IOPtics

`ioptics/algorithms/spec.py`'s `AlgorithmSpec` is built entirely around
parameterized least squares: `anw_model`, `bbnw_model`, `apriors`, `bpriors`,
`othera_priors`, `fit_method`, `mcmc`, `maxfev`, `beta`.  LS2 has none of that.
It is closed-form, solves every wavelength independently, reproduces the input
Rrs exactly by construction (so there is **no misfit, no χ²_ν, no ΔBIC and no
posterior coverage**), and it takes an observable BING never sees.  The metrics
and report stages assume those quantities exist.  This is the first
non-BING-shaped algorithm the framework has been asked to hold.

## Q&A

*Round 1, 2026-09-17, written from the thesis repository before the branch had
code.  Ordered by how much each changes the build.  Q1 and Q2 decide what is
actually being benchmarked; Q3–Q5 decide how; Q6–Q8 decide what "our own LS2"
finally means.*

**Q1. How far may the one-`register()` rule bend?**  The convention says adding
an algorithm is one `register(...)` call with no core changes.  LS2 cannot be
that: it is closed-form, needs Kd as a second observable, and produces no
misfit, so `fit_method`, `mcmc`, `apriors`/`bpriors` and every χ²-derived metric
are meaningless for it.  Three ways out, in increasing order of honesty and
cost: (a) a shim that wraps LS2 in a fake one-parameter "fit" so the existing
pipeline runs unchanged — cheap, and it will produce nonsense in the χ² columns;
(b) a second spec type (`DirectSpec`) and a `register_direct()` entry point,
with the run stage branching once on which kind it has and the metrics stage
emitting NaN for the fit-quality family — a real but contained core change;
(c) a separate runner and report for non-fitting algorithms, which duplicates
the metrics code.  *Recommended:* (b).  It is the change that makes IOPtics a
benchmarking framework rather than a BING harness, and the thesis wants exactly
that comparison.

**Q2. `b_p` is the hidden parameterization — which rung of the ladder do we
run?**  `ocpy`'s LS2 takes `b_p` as an argument; operational LS2 derives it from
Chl via OC4v4.  On L23 we can hand it truth.  That gives three rungs:
(i) **true Kd + true b_p** — what LS2 can do at its best, and an upper bound
nobody could reach from orbit; (ii) **true Kd + OC4v4 b_p** — isolates the cost
of the Chl side-chain; (iii) **NN Kd + OC4v4 b_p** — satellite mode, the only
rung that is a fair comparison with BING, which sees Rrs alone.  *Recommended:*
all three, on L23, reported as a ladder.  The steps between the rungs are the
result — they say how much of LS2's advertised accuracy is really an accuracy
of its inputs.  This needs a Chl→b_p function (Loisel & Morel 1998 with the
Morel & Maritorena 2001 slope) added to `ocpy`, which does not have one.  Do you
want it in `ocpy` or local to IOPtics?

**Q3. Does the Kd network's MODIS band set disqualify rung (iii)?**
`Kd_NN_MODIS` needs Rrs at 443, 488, 531, 547 and 667 nm.  L23 is hyperspectral
at 81 bands so those five can be sampled exactly, and PANGAEA's satellite-band
products include them — so it is *runnable*.  But it is a MODIS-trained network
being fed L23 Hydrolight spectra and PACE-like data, it is not the architecture
the 2018 paper describes, and the paper's own numbers say switching from true Kd
to NN Kd roughly doubles the error (7–8% → ≈15% MAPD).  *Recommended:* run it,
and report the true-Kd and NN-Kd rungs side by side rather than picking one.  If
you would rather we re-train a Kd network on L23, say so now — that is a
different and larger project and I would put it under the re-derivation in Q6,
not here.

**Q4. Which L23 inelastic variant, and do we run more than one?**  RT-A used
X=4 (+fluorescence).  LS2's LUTs were fit to Raman-free RT (X=1 is the match)
and its κ correction was fit to a Raman-only Case-1 set (X=2 is the match);
neither set contains fluorescence at all, so X=4 is *outside* everything LS2 was
derived for.  LS2 is closed-form and therefore nearly free to run.
*Recommended:* all three, X=1, 2 and 4, with κ off for X=1 and on for X=2 and 4.
That gives an inelastic-sensitivity result for LS2 at almost no cost and makes
the comparison with RT-A exact on X=4.  If you want only one, X=4, for
comparability with RT-A.

**Q5. What does "ok" mean for an algorithm with no misfit?**  The metrics stage
currently reports `frac_ok`, `frac_poor_fit`, `chi2_nu`, `rel_misfit`, `cov68`
and ΔBIC.  For LS2, χ²_ν, `rel_misfit`, `cov68` and ΔBIC are all undefined —
LS2 reproduces Rrs exactly and has no posterior.  What remains is: did it return
a number at all (the code sets `a_nw` or `bb_p` to NaN when the solution goes
negative, and κ to NaN outside the LUT's bb/a range), and how far is that number
from truth.  *Recommended:* define `frac_ok` for direct algorithms as "all
requested outputs finite and positive", record the NaN reasons separately
(negative `a_nw`, negative `bb_p`, κ out of range) because those are themselves
a result, emit NaN for the fit-quality family, and restrict head-to-head against
BING to the components both produce: `a`, `a_nw`, `bb`, `bb_p`.  **Not**
`a_ph`/`a_dg` — LS2 has none, and that absence is the thesis point, so it should
appear in the report as an explicit "not applicable" row rather than as a
missing column.

**Q6. What exactly gets re-derived, and from what?**  "Our own LS2" most
naturally means refitting the coefficients from our own radiative transfer
instead of inheriting Hydrolight-plus-Petzold.  The candidates are the a-LUT
(21×8×4), the bb-LUT (21×8×3), and the κ-LUT (101×7).  Two things make this
genuinely *ours* rather than a reproduction: the 2018 paper's accepted
(b/a, η) pair list is unpublished, so exact reproduction is impossible from the
paper alone; and L23 uses different particle phase functions from the single
Petzold average the LUTs were fit to, which the paper flags as the main
untested sensitivity for the bb coefficients.  *Recommended:* refit the a-LUT
and bb-LUT from L23 X=1 on the paper's (η, μw) grid, then refit κ from the
X=1 versus X=2 pair — which is a cleaner κ than the paper's, because it is a
matched Raman-on/Raman-off difference on the same water bodies.  Leave the Kd
network alone.  Do you want this inside the exam window or as "where we expect
to go"?

**Q7. Does the RoB forward model emit what a re-derivation needs?**  A
re-derivation from RoB rather than from L23 would be the stronger result — it is
the same move as RT-A, applied to a closed-form algorithm — but it requires the
model to produce E_d, E_u and Kd, not just Rrs.  I have not verified that it
can.  Should planning prompt 1 check, or is L23 the intended corpus?

**Q8. Attribution.**  The `ocpy` port is yours and Claude's from 2023-06-22 and
predates the thesis; the LUTs and the Kd network are Loisel, Stramski, Kehrli
and Reynolds'.  What is a thesis contribution here is the IOPtics integration,
the b_p/Kd ladder of Q2, and the re-derivation of Q6.  Confirm that is the line
you want drawn, and whether the chapter should say the port is yours.
